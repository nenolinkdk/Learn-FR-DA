# Linguistic Design --- French to Danish

## Direction

The learner is francophone. French provides explanations and semantic
support; Danish is the language to acquire and use.

## Method

Prioritise communicative situations over grammar sequencing. Each lesson
should have a small scenario and approximately ten distinct
dialogue/communication units.

Avoid mechanical repetition across lessons. Do not repeatedly make every
dialogue ask for help, missing information, or returning tomorrow.
Repetition is acceptable when pedagogically useful, but must be
controlled.

## Danish quality

Use natural contemporary Danish rather than literal French-to-Danish
transfer. Prefer what a user is likely to hear in Denmark, including
realistic references to apps and digital processes.

## Contrastive FR→DA focus

Potential explanatory topics: - en / et - noun definiteness - plural
formation - adjective agreement - personal pronouns - Danish informal
address and limited use of formal De - simple present - infinitive and
at - modal verbs - perfect tense - V2 word order - inversion - position
of ikke - question formation - der / det - man - common prepositions -
numbers and dates - pronunciation and reduced spoken forms

## Register

Tourist material should be polite, simple and usable. Work/living
material may gradually include more everyday and workplace register.
Children's material must sound natural for children and remain clear in
safety situations.

Dialogue and practical spoken French should sound like language a
French speaker would naturally use or recognise. Grammar examples and
reference notes may keep a more written or pedagogical register.
Do not convert every correct inversion into colloquial French; keep
inversion where it is conventional, useful, or belongs in grammar.

## Danish word order: *ikke*

In a **main clause**, *ikke* normally follows the finite verb:

`Jeg tror ikke, at toget kommer i dag.` (*ikke* negates *tror*)

In a **subordinate clause** (*at*, *fordi*, *hvis*, *når*, *som*, …),
*ikke* precedes the finite verb:

`Jeg tror, at toget ikke kommer i dag.`

The app must not teach that *ikke* always follows the finite verb.

## Notes

JSON should be able to carry optional: - grammar notes - vocabulary
notes - culture notes - digital notes - pronunciation notes

Notes should be concise and in French unless there is a strong reason
otherwise.

## TTS

Danish is the primary TTS language. French playback should remain
technically possible. Text intended for TTS should avoid markup that
breaks speech output.

## QA

Check: - semantic equivalence - natural Danish - natural French -
register - consistency - realistic Danish context - special characters:
æ ø å é è ê ë à â ç œ - TTS suitability
