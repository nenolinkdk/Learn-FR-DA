# Lesson Structure

## Level 1 --- Visiting Denmark

Provisional ten-lesson architecture:

1.  Arriving in Denmark
2.  Airport and railway station
3.  Train, metro and bus
4.  Hotel and accommodation
5.  Café
6.  Restaurant
7.  Shopping and payment
8.  Directions and sightseeing
9.  Tickets, travel apps and practical digital services
10. Problems and getting help

Each lesson should contain a small scenario and approximately ten
distinct dialogue/communication units.

## Level 2 --- Living and working in Denmark

Provisional architecture:

1.  Home and neighbourhood
2.  Supermarket and everyday shopping
3.  Workplace and colleagues
4.  Working day, meetings and appointments
5.  Doctor, pharmacy and health
6.  Municipality and public services
7.  MitID, Digital Post and digital Denmark
8.  Banking, cards and payments
9.  Commuting and transport
10. Social life and leisure

## Children / Enfants

This is a separate module. Final lesson count can be decided after the
adult architecture is validated.

Candidate situations: - meeting another child - name and age - asking
someone to play - playground - school - after-school activities -
football/sports - swimming pool - library - café/shop - clothes -
computer/internet/games - asking where something is - being lost -
hotel/holiday accommodation - beach - public transport - finding
parents - simple emergencies

## Dialogue design rule

A lesson is not ten unrelated phrase pairs. It should feel like a small
event unfolding in a specific place. The ten units may include
questions, answers, reactions, practical instructions and short
exchanges.

## Digital integration

Digital Denmark should appear where it naturally belongs: ticket
purchase in transport lessons, MobilePay/payment cards in shopping,
health card in health, MitID/Digital Post in public services, etc.
